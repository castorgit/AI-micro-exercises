Teaching note · for the instructor

10Jagged Frontier & Triage Mapper

⏱ 12–15 min live · ~20 min with debriefModel behavior & foundationsFramework · Augment Automate Protect Triage
Purpose

Because AI capability is jagged, you cannot allocate work by gut — you need tested capability on one axis and the consequence of a quiet failure on the other, which together dictate whether a task should be augmented, automated, or protected. Students map ten business tasks on that grid using revealed evidence, assign a triage label to each, then face a surprise model upgrade that should move some decisions. The aha is that the most dangerous task is the high-capability, high-consequence one whose failures are hard to detect — and that the map is perishable. This is a defensible, auditable method for deciding what to hand to AI and how much oversight to keep.

Concepts to explain & how

Two axes, not one: capability and consequence

How: Build the map live — x is tested capability, y is the consequence of a quiet failure. A task is only safe to automate when it is high-capability and low-consequence; everything else keeps a human.

Detectability decides the danger

How: Add the detectability lens: a confident, high-capability task whose errors slip through unnoticed is the trap. Ask which mapped task would fail silently and cost the most.

Augment, automate, or protect is the output

How: Convert each map position into a label — augment (human-led with AI help), automate (AI-led, low stakes), protect (keep human, high stakes or low detectability). The grid location, not opinion, assigns it.

The map expires

How: Trigger the surprise model upgrade and ask which tasks now move. Say: capability shifts, so the frontier map is a dated document, not a permanent policy.

Common misconceptions & where students get stuck
  • "High capability means safe to automate." A high-capability, high-consequence, low-detectability task is the most dangerous of all — the map places it in protect, not automate, and the exercise surfaces it deliberately.
  • "Once mapped, the allocation is settled." The surprise upgrade moves the frontier, so students who treated the map as permanent must redo placements and feel why retests have to be scheduled.
  • "Consequence is just the size of the impact." It is impact combined with how likely a failure is to go unnoticed — a small error that compounds silently can outrank a big visible one.
Facilitation & timing
  • 0:00 — Hand teams ten tasks; have them predict capability before Revealing the evidence drawer.
  • 3:00 — Teams score consequence and detectability and place each task on the two-axis map.
  • 7:00 — Teams assign augment/automate/protect from map position, not instinct.
  • 10:00 — Trigger the model-upgrade state; ask which decisions should move and why.
  • 12:00 — Compare maps across teams; open Debrief prompts on 'which high-confidence task was most dangerous?' and 'how often should the map expire?' Reset for the next group.
What mastery looks like

A student who gets it can place a new task on the capability–consequence map, assign augment/automate/protect with a reason, and say what evidence or model change would move it. Takeaway: turn jagged capability into a defensible allocation by mapping tested capability against consequence and detectability — and retest, because the frontier moves.
