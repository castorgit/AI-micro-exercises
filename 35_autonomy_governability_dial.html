Instructor reveal — autonomy is not one dial:
  • It's a dial per consequence class. Reading an invoice is reversible and cheap — full autonomy costs nothing. Paying €18,400 is irreversible — that dial belongs low, forever.
  • Low ≠ safe. "Approve everything" produced 200+ clicks a day, and by Wednesday the manager was rubber-stamping — including the duplicate. Drowning oversight is fake oversight.
  • High ≠ mature. Full autonomy looked impressive until the bad invoice got paid at machine speed. Maturity is a per-action mix that beats every single global setting — governable autonomy, not maximal autonomy.

Teaching note · for the instructor

35Autonomy–Governability Dial

⏱ 8–12 min live · ~15 min with debriefWorkflows, agents & production systemsFramework · Autonomy Governability Balance
Purpose

'Autonomy' gets treated as one dial and as a maturity badge, and both are wrong. This simulation splits autonomy into five separate freedoms — goals, tools, spending, communication and stopping — and shows that each freedom you grant adds capability but also a governance burden you then have to carry. Students learn to justify the minimum autonomy a mission actually needs rather than maxing the dials for prestige. The business relevance is that every freedom you grant an agent is a monitoring and liability cost, so governable mission fit beats raw autonomy.

Concepts to explain & how

Autonomy is a bundle of freedoms, not a single slider

How: Introduce the five dials separately and raise only one at a time; show the capability, variability, cost and control-requirement readouts moving independently.

Each freedom you grant carries a governance burden

How: Turn up the spending or tools dial and point at the control-requirement gauge rising in lockstep; you don't get the capability without also buying the oversight.

Aim for minimum autonomy for the mission, not maximum

How: Run the surprise scenario, then have teams revise downward; per the choreography, reward the team that hits the goal with the fewest freedoms unlocked.

Common misconceptions & where students get stuck
  • "Higher autonomy is more advanced." The scoring rewards governable mission fit, not maxed dials; a fully autonomous agent on a simple mission is over-built and under-controlled.
  • "Monitoring equals control." The debrief flags controls that are merely observational — you can watch a runaway without being able to stop it, and a dashboard is not a brake.
  • "Some freedoms are free." Every dial you raise moves the cost and control-requirement readouts, so there is no capability gain without an oversight cost.
Facilitation & timing
  • 0:00 — Pick a mission. Ask teams to guess which single freedom will matter most before touching the dials.
  • 2:00 — Teams set the five autonomy dials and watch capability, variability, cost and control requirements respond.
  • 5:00 — Instructor injects the surprise scenario; let teams feel where their settings help or hurt.
  • 8:00 — Teams revise, aiming for the minimum autonomy that still completes the mission.
  • 10:00 — Open Debrief prompts: which freedom created value, which monitoring was merely observational, and what should stay impossible? Reset for the next mission.
What mastery looks like

A student who gets it can decompose an agent's autonomy into specific freedoms, name the governance burden each one adds, and defend the least-autonomy design that still accomplishes the mission. Takeaway: autonomy is a bundle of separately granted freedoms, each with an oversight cost, so grant the minimum the mission needs and keep some things impossible by design.
